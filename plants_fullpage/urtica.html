Urtica

Urticaceae


Light

| Medium/High

Nettles can grow in full sun to partial shade. They prefer bright, indirect light but can tolerate various light conditions.

Water

| Medium/High

Keep the soil consistently moist but not waterlogged. Nettles prefer well-drained soil and will thrive with regular watering, especially during dry periods.

Soil

| Well-Drained

Nettles prefer rich, fertile soil high in organic matter. They thrive in nitrogen-rich environments and can improve soil fertility over time.

Humidity

| Medium

Nettles are hardy plants that can tolerate a range of temperatures. They thrive in temperate climates and prefer moderate humidity levels.


Nettle, commonly referred to as Stinging Nettle, is a hardy perennial herbaceous plant known for its stinging hairs and medicinal properties. The genus Urtica includes around 45 species, with Urtica dioica being the most well-known. Nettles are appreciated for their nutritional value and historical uses in traditional medicine.


Fertilization

Nettles generally do not require additional fertilization if grown in nutrient-rich soil. However, they can benefit from the occasional application of compost or organic fertilizer to boost growth.

Maintenance

Prune nettles regularly to control their spread and maintain a manageable size. Wear gloves and protective clothing when handling nettles to avoid stings. Remove any yellow or damaged leaves to keep the plant healthy.

Physical Characteristics

Nettles are herbaceous plants with serrated, heart-shaped leaves and tiny greenish flowers. They are covered with stinging hairs that release histamines and other chemicals upon contact, causing a stinging sensation. The plants can grow between 2 to 7 feet tall, depending on the species and growing conditions.

Common Names

Stinging Nettle, Common Nettle, Burn Nettle, Urtica, Tall Nettle

Order: Rosales | Family: Urticaceae | Genus: Urtica


Common Species

1. Urtica dioica (Common Nettle):
- Tall, robust plant with stinging hairs and heart-shaped leaves.
- Widely distributed across Europe, Asia, and North America.

2. Urtica urens (Dwarf Nettle):
- Smaller species with more intense stinging hairs.
- Found in Europe and introduced to North America and Australia.

3. Urtica ferox (Tree Nettle):
- Large, woody plant native to New Zealand with powerful stinging hairs.

4. Urtica gracilis (Slender Nettle):
- Native to North America with more delicate leaves and stems.

5. Urtica pilulifera (Roman Nettle):
- Notable for its round, spiky flower clusters.
- Found in the Mediterranean region.
