Hydrangea

Hydrangeaceae


Light

| Medium/High

Hydrangea plants prefer partial shade but can tolerate full sun in cooler climates. Too much direct sunlight can scorch their leaves, while too little light can reduce flowering.

Water

| Medium/High

Keep the soil consistently moist but not waterlogged. Hydrangea plants have high water needs, especially during blooming periods. Water deeply and regularly to maintain soil moisture.

Soil

| Well-Drained

Use a well-draining potting mix rich in organic matter. The soil pH can affect flower color in some species, with more acidic soil producing blue flowers and alkaline soil producing pink flowers.

Humidity

| Medium

Hydrangea plants thrive in mild, humid environments. Ideal temperatures range from 50°F to 70°F (10°C to 21°C). Protect them from extreme cold and hot temperatures.


Hydrangea, commonly referred to as Hortensia or Hydrangea, is a captivating genus of flowering plants known for their stunning and diverse blooms. The genus includes around 70-75 species, each boasting unique flower shapes, sizes, and colors. Hydrangea plants are prized for their ornamental value and are popular both as garden shrubs and potted plants.


Fertilization

Feed Hydrangea plants with a balanced, slow-release fertilizer in the spring and mid-summer. Avoid over-fertilizing, as this can lead to lush foliage but fewer flowers.

Maintenance

Prune Hydrangea plants to remove dead or damaged stems and to shape the plant. Pruning times can vary by species; some bloom on old wood and should be pruned after flowering, while others bloom on new wood and can be pruned in late winter or early spring.

Physical Characteristics

Hydrangea plants are known for their beautiful and varied blooms. The flowers can be large and spherical or flat and lace-like, with colors ranging from white and pink to blue and purple, often influenced by soil pH. The leaves are typically large, serrated, and can vary in color from deep green to red in the fall.

Common Names

Hortensia, Hydrangea

Order: Cornales | Family: Hydrangeaceae | Genus: Hydrangea


Common Species

1. Hydrangea macrophylla (Bigleaf Hydrangea):
- Features large, rounded flower clusters in shades of blue, pink, or white.
- Ideal for garden borders, prefers partial shade and moist, well-draining soil.

2. Hydrangea paniculata (Panicle Hydrangea):
- Known for its cone-shaped flower clusters that transition from white to pink.
- Thrives in full sun to partial shade and is more drought-tolerant than other species.

3. Hydrangea arborescens (Smooth Hydrangea):
- Notable for its large, white flower clusters and smooth leaves.
- Requires partial shade and well-draining soil, perfect for woodland gardens.

4. Hydrangea quercifolia (Oakleaf Hydrangea):
- Dramatic oak-shaped leaves and white flower clusters that age to pink.
- Prefers full shade to partial sun and is known for its stunning fall foliage.

5. Hydrangea serrata (Mountain Hydrangea):
- Features smaller, lace-cap flowers and serrated leaves.
- Grows best in partial shade and is more cold-hardy than other species.

6. Hydrangea petiolaris (Climbing Hydrangea):
- Has white, lace-cap flowers and can climb walls or trees with its aerial roots.
- Ideal for shaded areas and can tolerate a range of soil conditions.

7. Hydrangea anomala subsp. petiolaris (Japanese Climbing Hydrangea):
- Similar to the climbing hydrangea but with slightly smaller flowers.
- Prefers partial to full shade and well-draining soil.
