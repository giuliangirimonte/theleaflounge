Olea

Oleaceae


Light

| Medium/High

Olive trees require full sun to thrive. They need at least 6 hours of direct sunlight per day to produce fruit and maintain healthy growth.

Water

| Low

While olive trees are drought-tolerant, they benefit from regular watering, especially during the first few years of growth. Water deeply and infrequently, allowing the soil to dry out between watering sessions.

Soil

| Well-Drained

Olive trees prefer well-draining soil with a pH between 6 and 8. They can tolerate poor, rocky soils but perform best in fertile, slightly alkaline soils.

Humidity

| High

Olive trees thrive in warm, dry climates. Ideal temperatures range from 60°F to 85°F (15°C to 29°C). They can tolerate occasional frost but prolonged exposure to temperatures below 15°F (-9°C) can damage the trees.


Olive trees, scientifically known as Olea europaea, are evergreen trees native to the Mediterranean region, Asia, and parts of Africa. They are well-known for their silvery-green foliage and their fruit, olives, which are a staple in Mediterranean cuisine. Olive trees have been cultivated for thousands of years and are celebrated for their economic, cultural, and culinary significance.


Fertilization

Feed olive trees with a balanced fertilizer in the spring and fall. Avoid excessive nitrogen, as it can lead to lush foliage at the expense of fruit production.

Maintenance

Prune olive trees annually to maintain their shape, remove dead or diseased wood, and improve air circulation. This helps reduce the risk of pests and diseases.

Physical Characteristics

Olive trees have a gnarled, twisted trunk and can grow up to 50 feet tall. The leaves are narrow, elongated, and silvery-green, providing a striking contrast against the tree's dark, rough bark. The small, white flowers bloom in clusters, leading to the development of the fruit.

Common Names

Olive, European Olive

Order: Lamiales | Family: Oleaceae | Genus: Olea | Species: O. europaea


Common Species

1. Arbequina:
- Small, brownish-purple olives with a mild, fruity flavor.
- Highly productive and suitable for oil production.

2. Kalamata:
- Large, dark purple olives with a rich, meaty texture.
- Commonly used as table olives.

3. Manzanilla:
- Small to medium-sized, green olives with a slightly nutty flavor.
- Popular as table olives and in salads.

4. Picual:
- Medium-sized, green to black olives with a strong, bitter flavor.
- Primarily used for oil production.

5. Picholine:
- Small, elongated green olives with a firm texture.
- Often used as cocktail olives.

6. Mission:
- Small to medium-sized, black olives with a mild, slightly sweet flavor.
- Versatile, used for both oil and table olives.
