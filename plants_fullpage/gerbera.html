Gerbera

Asteraceae


Light

| Medium/High

Prefers bright, indirect sunlight. Ideal conditions are 6-8 hours of bright, indirect light per day. Avoid harsh, direct sunlight which can scorch the leaves and flowers. A south or west-facing window with filtered light works best.

Water

| Medium/High

Keep the soil consistently moist but avoid overwatering. Water when the top inch of soil feels dry. Water at the base of the plant to avoid wetting the leaves, which can cause fungal issues.

Soil

| Well-Drained

Use a well-draining, nutrient-rich potting mix. A combination of potting soil, peat moss, perlite, and sand. Slightly acidic to neutral soil (pH 5.5 - 6.5).

Humidity

| Medium

65°F to 75°F (18°C to 24°C) during the day and not below 45°F (7°C) at night. Avoid cold drafts, frost, and extreme heat. Moderate humidity is preferred, but Gerbera can adapt to normal household humidity levels.


Gerbera, known for its vibrant and cheerful daisy-like flowers, is a popular ornamental plant admired for its wide array of colors and its ability to brighten up any garden or indoor space. Native to South Africa, these perennials are prized for their striking blooms and long-lasting cut flowers.


Fertilization

Feed Gerbera regularly to promote healthy growth and abundant blooms every 2-4 weeks during the growing season (spring through fall). Balanced, water-soluble fertilizer or a bloom-enhancing fertilizer high in potassium. Avoid over-fertilizing, which can cause salt buildup and damage roots.

Maintenance

Remove spent flowers promptly to encourage continuous blooming. Remove yellow or damaged leaves to maintain the plant’s health and appearance. Repot every 1-2 years to refresh the soil and provide ample room for root growth.

Physical Characteristics

Gerbera produces large, single or double-petaled flowers in shades of red, orange, yellow, pink, white, and purple. The leaves are typically lobed, green, and form a rosette at the plant’s base. Stems are long and sturdy, making them ideal for cut flowers.

Common Names

Gerbera Daisy, Transvaal Daisy, Barberton Daisy, African Daisy

Order: Asterales | Family: Asteraceae | Genus: Gerbera | Species: Gerbera jamesonii (most common species)


Common Species

1. Gerbera jamesonii:
- The most common variety, known for its vivid, classic daisy blooms.

2. Gerbera garvinea:
- A hardy variety with excellent disease resistance and prolonged blooming period.

3. Gerbera ‘Revolution’ Series:
- Compact, early-flowering plants perfect for containers.

4. Gerbera ‘Everlast’ Series:
- Known for its continuous blooming and robust nature.
